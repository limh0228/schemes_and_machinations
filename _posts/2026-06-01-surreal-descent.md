<br>

In June, a dear friend and writing partner of mine, Tomás, died suddenly from a congenital health condition. No one I loved had ever died before, and the pain from this was a strong enough catalyst to break me like a twix. The following period of my surreal, psychic distress and departure from reality co-existed the 2026 FIFA world cup finals games in NYC and the prisoners’ hunger strike at Delaney Hall ICE detention center just 11 miles away in New Jersey, which is a very unique experience I can only describe as k-holing at the evil circus. Though to be fair I had been spiraling for months already and I think my mad descent really began in January. 

<br>

At that time, I had been working as a software engineer for five years in Microsoft’s cloud service, Azure. I worked on a core algorithmic component which allocated datacenter resources for customers’ requested virtual machines; This sentence is so distilled of major complexity, that it is almost a lie, but since I didn’t read my NDA very carefully, I will leave it at that. 

<br>

One fateful weekend in January, I went to a deportation defense rapid response training where I learned how to respond to an ICE sighting in New York City. We split up and sat according to our boroughs of residence, and listened to a 3 hour power-point presentation from two activists using aliased names Salt and Pepper (which have been changed for anonymity, but imagine something equally as cute). We reviewed publicly published photos of detainment in action to practice visually distinguishing the ICE agents (to avoid mixing them up with the NYPD, the National Guard, and other non-ICE law enforcement actors). They don’t seem to have any uniform requirements. Many of the agents wore plain clothes with lazy and potent outlines of their guns and vests underneath. Most did not have badges or other visible markers of identification. Many bore the military build marked by broad shoulders, stout torso, and tense posture – a body, decidedly masculine, constructed as an interchangeable and anonymous tool and weapon of the state. All of the agents wore face masks. We saw one agent wearing a black mask emblazoned with the Nazi Totenkopf skull. In all the images, the detainees faces were blurred.

<br>

Because ICE agents are famously unvetted, untrained, and trigger-happy, and their violation of, and disregard for civilian rights is so ubiquitous, we were encouraged during rapid response to take videos of the detainment in action for later use in filing post-detainment habeas corpus petitions (judicial orders requesting law enforcement authorities to produce a prisoner they are holding, and to justify the prisoner's continued confinement). ICE officers are so unfamiliar with the law, in fact, that I originally wrote the bulk of this section using the word “detainment” and I had to edit most of those usages out in post where they read like full-blooded satire. It is also a well-documented fact that people “detained” by ICE disappear in the system, and though many ICE detainments are single collections targeting specific people who are asked or tricked to produce proof of their identity, an ICE agent does not need a warrant to perform an arrest, and many detainments are only legally justified ad-hoc by a very loose set of standards including country of origin, language, or occupation, to produce “reasonable suspicion” that someone is an illegal immigrant. 

<br>

This was not always the case, but in September of 2025, the U.S. Supreme Court, in a 6-3 decision on Noem v. Vasquez-Perdomo, lifted a lower court ban on ICE’s “roving patrols” in Los Angeles. The ACLU of Wisconsin elaborated: “What does that mean in plain language? This means that ICE agents now have the legal cover to stop, question, and detain people based on appearance, language, accent, workplace, or any other marker that “looks suspicious” to them. In other words, the Court just gave its blessing to racial profiling on a massive scale—and they did it without a full briefing or hearing and without explaining their reasoning.” 

<br>

Even though we did not actually learn or do anything illegal at the training (in fact mostly, we learned a lot about what exactly IS legal, and talked at length about curtilage trespassing law), I felt as if I was at huge risk at that time, knowing all of our phones were broadcasting our locations, and considering that the administration seemed only passively interested in legality as a structure capable of enabling the terms of its own brutality and agenda. Research shows that personalized paranoia is one of the two most common schizophrenic conspiracies (grandeur being the other), but having anxious delusions of persecution in a burgeoning fascist regime is like being a hypochondriac during COVID. 

<br>

I brought a notebook and a pen, and six of my friends. We talked through grassroots resistance strategies such as yelling, using a whistle, passively forming crowds around vehicles, public shaming, informing detainees of their rights, and we were instructed to remember, above all, that the person being kidnapped is being kidnapped. We studied a photo of one woman with her hands held behind her back, pressed against a car by a man in a black mask and sunglasses. Both people in the photo were visually beheaded: faces covered, pixelated, masked, or abstracted. The man wore jeans and a tactical vest. The photo showed her bent at an angle, her body, modified by an acute and multi-dimensional violence. I had assumed her face was blurred for privacy, but did not consider that it might be blurred as gore. “Is this ICE?” we were asked. We were not sure.

<br>

After the training the six of us went to a nearby bar where I tried not to cry. At the time, I was mainly confused at the idea that I was the one who would have to stop someone from getting kidnapped. I’m 5’2”.

<br>

After this, a group emergent from my local mutual aid network began morning and evening walks to protect our own neighborhoods from the kidnappings, and to identify large staging areas for raids where caravans of unmarked rental vans could theoretically gather to strategize a mass abduction. In the mornings, I would walk loops or grids around my neighborhood, and then around 8 AM, I would sit down at my desk to answer emails for the very same empire.
